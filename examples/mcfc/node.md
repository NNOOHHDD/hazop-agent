# MCFC stack

Scope: the stack section of a molten carbonate fuel cell (MCFC) plant that supplies electric propulsion power on a liquefied-hydrogen carrier. The node starts at the three inlet streams (air; natural gas mixed with water, called "feed"; hydrogen boil-off gas), passes through the internal reformer, the anode and the cathode, and ends at the cathode, anode and vent outlets and at the DC power output. The drawing shows all stack modules as one segment.

Design intent and key facts:
- The stack runs at about 650 °C with a molten carbonate salt as the electrolyte.
- The feed is reformed inside the stack (steam methane reforming and water-gas shift) into H2, CO and CO2.
- At the anode, H2 and CO react with carbonate ions and release electrons; at the cathode, O2 and CO2 take up the electrons and form carbonate ions again.
- The cell reaction gives off heat; the endothermic reforming reaction is what keeps the stack in heat balance.
- The wider plant has many stack modules plus compressors for air, hydrogen and natural gas, a water pump and several heat exchangers.
- A basic process control system (BPCS) controls cell voltage and its deviation, stack and exhaust temperature, current, air flow and pressure, and fuel flow, pressure and temperature. Control and monitoring follow classification society rules.
